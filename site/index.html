What changed in the notes

The organization fixes

  • Elasticity is no longer split across weeks. Price, cross-price, income and supply elasticity now sit inside the demand chapter, immediately after demand shifters and before consumer choice.
  • Monopoly is one chapter. Week 4's introduction is merged with every worked problem from the Week 12 final review: tables, graphs, units, DWL, efficient pricing and fair-returns pricing.
  • Growth is a sequence. Week 7 (why countries are rich) → Week 9 (how they grow) → Week 10 (why some succeed). The notes keep that causal chain.
  • Policy is rebuilt around three variables. For every tool, ask what happens to output, jobs and inflation. That is the professor's own final-review framework.
  • Admin removed. No feedback forms, group presentation reminders or course wrap-up slides.

BlockTimeWhat to do
MCQs (25)35–40 minOne pass. Flag anything longer than 90 seconds. No blank answers.
Written (25)75–80 min~3 min each. Formula → substitution → result → one sentence of interpretation.
Review5 minCheck units, per-unit vs total, and whether the question asked for a number or an explanation.
Highest-value habit

Before calculating, underline the units and the words per unit, total, real or nominal. Most lost marks in the midterm came from reading the question too quickly, not from not knowing the economics.

Course conventions used on this site

Match the professor's method.

Percentage changes use the initial value as the base (as in the weekly quizzes). Profit-maximising output is where MR = MC; price is then read from demand. GDP deflator = nominal/real × 100. PPP uses the price ratio. The Rule of 70 uses the growth rate in percent.
