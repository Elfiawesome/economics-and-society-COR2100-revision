One-page compression

Formula sheet & graph recipes.

Everything that belongs on your handwritten A4. The right-hand column is the exam trigger: what the question says, and therefore what to reach for. Print this page or copy it onto your cheat sheet.

1 · Foundations

Opportunity costValue of the best alternative forgone (not the sum).
Net benefitBenefit − opportunity cost. Act if ≥ 0.
Compounded wagewt = w0(1 + g)t
Mean vs medianMean is pulled by outliers; median is the middle value.
CausationOmitted variable / reverse causality / natural experiment.

2 · Demand, supply, equilibrium

Linear demandQd = a − bP, or P = a − bQ.
Linear supplyQs = c + dP, or P = c + dQ.
EquilibriumSet Qd = Qs (or demand price = supply price).
ShortageQd − Qs when price is below equilibrium.
SurplusQs − Qd when price is above equilibrium.
Market demandHorizontal sum: add quantities at each price.

3 · Elasticity

Price elasticityEd = %ΔQd / %ΔP. Course uses initial values as the base.
Classification|Ed| > 1 elastic; = 1 unit; < 1 inelastic.
Point elasticityEd = (ΔQ/ΔP)(P/Q) = −b(P/Q) for Q = a − bP.
RevenueElastic: price ↑ → spending ↓. Inelastic: price ↑ → spending ↑.
Cross-priceEab = %ΔQa / %ΔPb. Positive = substitutes; negative = complements.
IncomeEI = %ΔQ / %ΔI. Negative = inferior; 0–1 necessity; >1 luxury.
Supply elasticityEs = %ΔQs / %ΔP.

4 · Consumer choice & surplus

BudgetPxX + PyY = I. Intercepts I/Px, I/Py.
Slope / opp. cost−Px/Py. Opportunity cost of X = Px/Py units of Y.
MB per dollarMBx/Px. Buy the next unit with the highest ratio.
OptimumMBx/Px = MBy/Py, and budget exhausted.
Consumer surplusCS = WTP − P. Market: area below demand, above price.
Linear CSCS = ½ × (demand intercept − P) × Q.

5 · Costs, profit, competition

CostsTC = FC + VC; ATC = TC/Q; AVC = VC/Q; AFC = FC/Q.
Marginal costMC = ΔTC/ΔQ. MC crosses ATC and AVC at their minima.
Profitπ = TR − TC = (P − ATC)Q. TR = P × Q.
Accounting vs economicAccounting subtracts explicit costs only; economic also subtracts implicit costs.
ShutdownProduce if P ≥ AVC; shut down if P < AVC. Fixed costs are irrelevant to shutdown.
Competitive supplyShort-run supply = MC above minimum AVC. P = MR = MC.
Producer surplusArea below price, above supply. Linear: PS = ½(P − supply intercept)Q.
Long runFree entry/exit → P = MC = ATC, zero economic profit.

6 · Monopoly & regulation

MRFor P = a − bQ, MR = a − 2bQ. MR < P.
Profit maxMR = MC → QM; then PM from the demand curve.
Profitπ = (P − ATC)Q, or TR − TC if the table gives exact TC.
WelfareCS = ½(a − PM)QM; PS = (PM − MC)QM (constant MC).
DWL½(PM − MC)(QC − QM), where QC solves P = MC.
Efficient priceP = MC; may cause a loss for a natural monopoly.
Fair-returns priceP = ATC; zero economic profit per unit.
Price / quantity effectCut price: gain P2ΔQ, lose (P1 − P2)Q1.

7 · Externalities, public goods, tax

Social costMSC = MPC + MEC.
Social benefitMSB = MPB + MEB.
Negative externalityMarket Q where D = MPC; social Q where D = MSC. Market overproduces.
Positive externalitySocial Q where MSB = MPC. Market underproduces.
Pigouvian taxTax = MEC at the social optimum. Subsidy = MEB.
Tax incidenceBurden falls more on the less elastic side. Statutory ≠ economic.
Consumer shareFor Qd = a − bP and Qs = c + dP: d/(b + d).
Tax revenue / DWLRevenue = tQ1; DWL = ½t(Q0 − Q1).
Public goodsNon-rival + non-excludable → free-rider problem → tax finance.

8 · Price controls

CeilingBinds if below equilibrium → shortage; quantity traded = Qs.
FloorBinds if above equilibrium → surplus; quantity traded = Qd.
Ceiling DWL½(Q0 − Qs)(Pd(Qs) − ceiling).
Floor DWL½(Q0 − Qd)(floor − Ps(Qd)).
No biteCeiling above equilibrium or floor below equilibrium has no effect.

9 · GDP, GNI, prices

Expenditure GDPGDP = C + I + G + (X − M).
Value addedSales − intermediate purchases. Sum across firms avoids double counting.
GNIGDP + income received from abroad − income paid abroad.
Nominal / realNominal = current prices × current quantities. Real = base prices × current quantities.
DeflatorNominal/real × 100. Real = nominal/(deflator/100).
CPI(current basket cost / base basket cost) × 100.
Inflation%Δ price index = (CPI2 − CPI1)/CPI1 × 100.
SavingS = Y − C − G. Closed: S = I. Open: S − I = NX.

10 · Productivity & PPP

GDP per capitaGDP/population.
Productivity identityGDP/pop = (GDP/hours)(hours/workers)(workers/pop).
Human capitalH = L × h.
Production functionY = A × F(K, H). Cobb–Douglas: Y = A K1/3 H2/3.
PPPGDPPPP = local GDP × (US price / local price).
Market conversionLocal GDP × exchange rate (US$ per unit).
Gap decompositionTotal gap = technology gap × K&H gap.

11 · Growth

Growth rateg = (yt − y0)/y0 × 100.
Exponential growthyt = (1 + g)t y0.
Average growthg = (yt/y0)1/t − 1.
Rule of 70Doubling time ≈ 70/g(%). Backwards: g ≈ 70/doubling time.
Growth accountingA = total − K − H. Share of A = A/total.
Catch-upfraction × (1 + gP)t = (1 + gL)t.
Catch-up timet = ln(1/fraction) / ln[(1 + gP)/(1 + gL)].
Saving / investmentClosed: S = I. Open: S − I = NX.

12 · Institutions & policy

EntrepreneurshipR(N) = a − bN. Enter until R = W: N* = (a − W)/b.
Weak property rightsReturn becomes sR → N = (sa − W)/b (leftward shift).
Entry barrierCost rises to W + E → N = (a − W − E)/b.
Real interest rater ≈ i − πe. Deflation raises r.
Expansionary fiscalG ↑, T ↓, transfers ↑ → output ↑, jobs ↑, inflation ↑.
Contractionary fiscalG ↓, T ↑, transfers ↓ → output ↓, jobs ↓, inflation ↓.
Fed easingBuy bonds → cash ↑ → interest rates ↓ → spending ↑.
MAS easingS$ strengthens more slowly/weakens → exports cheaper, imports dearer → output ↑, jobs ↑, inflation ↑.
MAS tighteningS$ strengthens faster → imports cheaper, exports dearer → output ↓, jobs ↓, inflation ↓.

Graph recipe: competition & monopoly

  • Competitive equilibrium: demand = supply; CS triangle above price below demand; PS triangle below price above supply.
  • Monopoly: MR = MC → go down to QM; go up to demand → PM; go down to ATC → cost. Profit rectangle = (PM − ATC)QM.
  • Monopoly DWL: triangle between demand and MC from QM to QC, where QC is where P = MC.
  • Natural monopoly: ATC falling over the relevant range; MC below ATC, so efficient pricing creates a loss.

Graph recipe: externalities, tax, controls

  • Negative externality: MSC above MPC by MEC. Market Q at D = MPC; social Q at D = MSC. DWL lies between them.
  • Positive externality: MSB above MPB by MEB. Market Q at MPB = MPC; social Q at MSB = MPC. DWL is the missing surplus from QM to QS.
  • Tax: vertical wedge t between demand and supply. Consumers pay the demand-side price; sellers keep the supply-side price. Revenue rectangle = t × Q1; DWL triangle on lost units.
  • Price ceiling: horizontal line below equilibrium; quantity traded on the supply curve; shortage to the right. Price floor: line above equilibrium; quantity traded on the demand curve; surplus to the right.
  • Labour market: wage floor above equilibrium → employment on labour demand; unemployment = Ls − Ld.
Exam trigger table

"Profit-maximising"MR = MCPrice from demand, not MR.
"Efficient / socially optimal price"P = MCMay be a loss for natural monopoly.
"Fair-returns price"P = ATCZero economic profit per unit.
"Deadweight loss"Triangle to the efficient quantityUse competitive Q, not monopoly Q.
"Externality optimum"Include MEC or MEBNegative → tax; positive → subsidy.

"Inflation"% change in a price indexCPI level ≠ inflation rate.

Top traps

The marks you can save

  1. Units: axis in thousands/hundreds; convert before profit or DWL.
  2. Per unit vs total: profit per unit is P − ATC; tax per bottle vs total tax.
  3. Profit vs revenue: profit subtracts cost.
  4. Movement vs shift: own price = movement; other determinants = shift.
  5. Elasticity base: use initial values, as the course does.
  6. Tax incidence: statutory who-pays does not matter; elasticity does.
  7. Shutdown: compare P with AVC, not ATC. Fixed costs do not affect the decision.
  8. Long run: zero economic profit, not zero accounting profit.
  9. Monopoly price: read from demand after finding MR = MC.
  10. DWL: right edge is where P = MC (or MSB = MSC), not QM.
  11. Externalities: negative overproduces, positive underproduces.
  12. GDP: excludes resale, transfers, intermediate goods, unpaid work; subtracts imports.
  13. GDP vs GNI: location vs residency.
  14. Nominal vs real: price changes vs quantity changes.
  15. CPI vs deflator: consumer basket incl. imports vs domestic output.
  16. PPP: price ratio, not exchange rate.
  17. Diminishing MP: MP falls before total output falls.
  18. Compounding: do not add growth rates linearly.
  19. Growth accounting: contributions are percentage points; shares are percentages.
  20. Catch-up: not automatic; use the ratio of (1+g), not the difference.
  21. Proximate vs fundamental: K/H/A vs geography/culture/institutions.
  22. Demand vs supply shock: inflation is the distinguishing variable.
  23. MAS: stronger S$ = contractionary; weaker/slower = expansionary.
  24. Real interest: subtract expected inflation; deflation raises the real rate.
  25. Correlation: do not claim causation without omitted variables / reverse causality / natural experiment.

  • Middle: costs + perfect competition + monopoly.
  • Right: externalities + public goods + tax + price controls.

Cheat-sheet rule

Write the trigger next to each formula: "who bears tax → elasticity", "DWL → efficient Q", "technology → residual". A formula without a trigger is hard to find under time pressure.
